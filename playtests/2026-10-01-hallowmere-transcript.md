Hallowmere sits in the hole where a lake used to be. The dike road runs in along the old shoreline and drops you twenty feet below the waterline of a century ago, into streets of packed grey silt that take a bootprint and keep it. Everything smells of wet iron. Eleven months you have carried three letters, and the third one was sent from here.

The Drowned Bell is the second building past the cut. Low beams, a hearth that smokes, and a cracked ship's bell nailed over it with the clapper gone. Nine or ten dike-crew drinking, and not one of them talking. They look at your chain, find the sawn-through medallion at your throat, and go back to their cups — but slower than they should.

The dwarf behind the bar does not pretend to be busy when you come to her. Grey hair, apron over an oilskin, arms folded. She lets you get all the way through the question before she answers it.

"Adela Fenn. I run it." A beat. "Hold a line. Right." She looks at the shoulders, the shield, the way you stand with your back off the room. "Wake-watch wants hands. Four out of the cut since the thaw — four bodies, I mean, in four months — and they've stopped pretending it's drink. Silver a day and board to walk the cut from dusk. Nobody local will take it." She wipes the bar in one slow pass. "Chapterhouse pays better. Pale Bell keeps a Preceptor here now. Man called Vane. He hires, if your sort of trouble doesn't follow you."

When you ask after rope and a lantern she tips her chin at the shelf behind her — crates stacked to the beam, dike-crew staples, the whole town buys here because there is nowhere else.

The six gold is most of what you have. Rope coiled on the hip, lantern on the strap. Then you put the real question on the bar between you and watch her face while she handles it — Insight [Insight], DC 15.

You misread her completely.

She goes still when you ask it, and you take the stillness for a confession — so you lean on it, and you are wrong. What is actually in her face is a woman who has had Order chain in her taproom before and did not enjoy it. The flatness is not a lid on a secret. It is the door closing.

"Nothing," Adela says. "That's the honest answer and you'll call it a lie, which is why I don't talk to your lot." She takes the cloth off the bar. "Bed's five silver, up the stair, and I'd rather you took it and slept. Ask the Wake-watch your questions. Ask Vane. Don't ask me what I'm keeping, in my own house, with my crews sat behind you listening."

They are listening. And one of them — a thin man in a dredger's waxed coat, who has been nursing the same cup since you walked in — sets it down unfinished, pulls his hood up, and goes out into the rain. Nobody at his table looks up, which tells you more than anything Adela said.

Three gold left in your purse. Rope on your hip, lantern on the strap, and the cracked bell over the hearth with no clapper in it.
