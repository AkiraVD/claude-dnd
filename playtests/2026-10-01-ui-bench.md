# UI bench, 2026-10-01 — mid-session surfaces

Not a playtest. A deliberate pass over the in-play UI with state driven straight
into the engine, to look at the panels rather than the fiction. Ran on a throwaway
campaign ("UI test bench", id 3) so the real one was never touched.

## What held up

- Scene panel, backdrop art (`tavern-5.webp` chosen from the scene text), character
  panel with HP bar, AC, the six ability boxes, hit dice and slot pips, and the
  attack list off the sheet.
- `add_creature` with `class_name: ranger` gave STR/DEX saves — the fix from the
  Hallowmere session is holding.
- The commoner fallback: Corvin Hask with no scores came out 4/4, AC 10.
- NPC portrait resolved to the stock library file and rendered beside the name.
- Vendor stock built with `give_item` + price, three lines, correct quantities.

## Finding 1 — the browser follows the wrong campaign after a resume (fixed)

`readTable` draws whatever `mostRecentCampaign()` returns, which is `listCampaigns()[0]`,
ordered by `updated_at`. `updated_at` is bumped in exactly one place: `appendStep`.
`load_campaign` only assigns `#campaign` in memory.

So resuming an older campaign left every panel — scene, character sheet, cast —
showing the *previously* played campaign until the first mutating tool call landed.
That window is the whole start of a session: the recap, the "last time you were…",
the first look at the sheet.

Reproduced live: engine on campaign 1, browser drawing campaign 3 with its character
and its cast.

Fixed by having `load_campaign` touch the campaign, since loading *is* playing.
`persistence.test.ts` pins it, and fails without the change.

## Finding 2 — "which campaign is loaded" is per-process, and nothing says so

There is no persisted current-campaign flag; each `Store` holds `#campaign` in its own
memory. Three processes can therefore disagree at once, which is what happened here:
my engine on campaign 3, the DM's engine still on campaign 2, the browser drawing
whichever row was written last.

In ordinary single-player use the DM is the only writer, so this stays invisible —
but it is only ever one extra writer away from surfacing, and the symptom is baffling
when it does. Finding 1's fix narrows it; it does not remove it.

Worth noting what the DM did when the ground moved: it called `get_state` and
`list_campaigns`, said plainly that the engine did not back up the premise, laid out
the three options, and refused to switch campaigns on its own initiative. That is the
prompt working exactly as intended under genuinely confusing conditions.

## The two surfaces that need a live DM turn

`offer_choice` and `open_shop` render from the WebSocket tool-result stream, not from
`/api/state`, so neither can be driven from outside the DM's own process. Tested on a
second pass with a real turn, on the bench campaign.

**The shop.** Header carries the profile line and the attitude badge. Stock lists each
item with its remaining count, its icon, and a stepper. The purse ledger reads
Purse / Basket / After and the arithmetic is exact in copper: a 50 gp potion against
12 gp 7 sp 4 cp reported "Short by 37 gp 2 sp 6 cp" — 5000 − 1274 = 3726 — and
disabled the buy button, which dims through colour rather than opacity. One rope and
two arrow bundles came to 3 gp, After 9 gp 7 sp 4 cp.

Completing it round-trips correctly: the composer sent the basket as an ordinary
message, the DM put it through `buy`, and the snapshot shows Vessa 1274 → 974 cp,
Hask 4000 → 4300, rope 3 → 2, arrows 4 → 2, the potion untouched, in one atomic step.

**The chips.** Six options rendered above the composer, including the dimmed
"something else". Clicking one *fills the composer rather than sending it*, so the
pick stays editable, and the row stays up so it can be changed. That is the
"a shortcut and never a cage" line actually honoured in the UI.

**Cards.** Only rolls and rejections get their own card; an ordinary successful call
is silent. Worth knowing before mistaking it for a dropped message — a `set_story`
that had plainly committed showed nothing, which looked like a bug for a while and
is not one.

**Timing.** The turn that built the story backbone took about four minutes with no
visible progress beyond the spinner. Nothing was wrong, but there is no signal
distinguishing "thinking hard" from "wedged", and four minutes is well past where a
player would start poking at it.

## Combat, and two bugs under it

Initiative, turn order, rounds advancing, damage reaching the HP bars, miss and hit
cards, rejected-tool cards and the scene updating mid-fight all work. Four creatures,
three rounds, Vreeke taken from 16/16 to 6/16 by a longbow.

The engine held the line twice without being asked to. An `attack_roll` naming a
weapon that did not exist came back *"Sabat has no attack called 'net'. Has: weighted
net, boarding axe"* — a rejection that also tells you the answer. And an attack the
fiction had already announced as connecting simply missed on the dice, because the
roll decides and the narration does not.

### Finding 3 — attack cards ran their words together (fixed)

Every attack card has read `longbowVessa → Vreeke` since attack cards existed. The
heading was JSX:

    {weapon ?? 'attack'}
    {String(call.input['attacker'] ?? '')} → {String(call.input['target'] ?? '')}

JSX discards whitespace spanning a line break, so the gap between `{weapon}` and
`{attacker}` was never there. The ` → ` survived only because it sits on the same
line as both of its neighbours.

Now a pure `attackLabel()` with its own test, in the spirit of the note at the top of
`setup.ts`: the composition is the part that can quietly go wrong, so it lives
outside the component where it can be asserted.

### Finding 4 — no .tsx file had ever been typechecked (fixed)

Found by accident: adding a test that imports `DiceCard.js` pulled the component into
the program and lit up errors that `npm run typecheck` had always reported clean.

The root `tsconfig.json` include was `packages/*/src/**/*.ts` — which does not match
`.tsx`. Every React component in the project was outside the typechecker. CLAUDE.md
says "strict TS across every package"; it was strict TS across every package except
the UI.

Including `.tsx` surfaced 33 errors. Thirty were one mechanical thing — React 19 drops
the global `JSX` namespace, so each file needs `import type { JSX } from 'react'`. The
other three were real: `Panels.tsx` builds a chip list by spreading
`creature.conditions.active`, which infers `SimpleCondition[]`, and then pushes
`'dead'`, `'dying' | 'stable'` and `` `exhaustion ${n}` `` onto it. None of those is a
`SimpleCondition`. Harmless at runtime, and exactly the class of thing the
typechecker is for.
